## Overview

An expansion panel hides a section behind a header, so people can open it when they need it. It keeps a screen short and calm: the things most people need sit on the screen itself, and the rest waits in a panel.

## When to use

Use an expansion panel for:

- Advanced options in a form
- The optional fields of a dialog
- A group of filters

Don't use an expansion panel for:

- What people need to complete the task. If someone has to open the panel every time, the content should not be in a panel.
- A set of rows that open something else. Use a [list](/design/components/list/).

## Anatomy

1. **Container**: with `outlined`, a border and rounded corners around the whole panel
2. **Header**: the row people select to open or close the panel
3. **Leading icon**: optional, an icon at the start of the header
4. **Title**: the header text
5. **Secondary text**: optional, a short line below the title
6. **Chevron**: points down when closed, and turns up when open. At the end of the header, or at the start with `left-chevron`.
7. **Header icons**: optional, actions at the end of the header
8. **Content**: the section that opens and closes

## Variants

### Header

The header says what is inside. Keep it short: **Advanced settings**, **Areas**, **Network**. A secondary line below it can give a short summary, like the current value or why someone would open it.

### Outlined

An outlined panel gets a border and rounded corners. The frontend uses it when the panel sits in a form or a dialog, next to other fields, so it reads as one block. The expandable section of a [form](/design/components/form/) and the card editors do this. Without the outline, the panel blends into its surroundings, which works in a list of panels, like the filter panes and the card picker.

### Chevron on the left

By default, the chevron sits at the end of the header. It can move to the start. The filter panes on the configuration pages use this, with a count of active filters and a button to clear them in the header.

<Demo layout="stack">
  <ha-expansion-panel left-chevron header="Areas">
    <p>Filter by area.</p>
  </ha-expansion-panel>
</Demo>

### Open by default

A panel can be open from the start.

<Demo layout="stack">
  <ha-expansion-panel outlined expanded header="Data to back up">
    <p>Settings and history, media, and the share folder.</p>
  </ha-expansion-panel>
</Demo>

### Always open

Without the chevron, people can no longer open or close the panel. Open from the start, it is a section that looks like a panel but always stays open.

## Behavior

- **Closed**: only the header shows. The content is not rendered at all.
- **Opening and closing**: selecting the header opens or closes the panel. The content slides open or closed in 300ms, and the chevron turns in 150ms.
- **Focus**: while the header has focus, it gets the input fill as background. With keyboard focus, the chevron also gets a round neutral fill.
- **Always open**: the header can't be selected or focused.

## Guidelines

- Follow what the frontend does: an outlined panel in a form or a dialog, next to other fields, and no outline in a list of panels, like the filter panes.

## Accessibility

### Keyboard

| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| <kbd>Tab</kbd> | Moves focus to the header. A panel that is always open is skipped. |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> | Opens or closes the panel. |

### Focus

While the header has focus, it gets the input fill as background. With keyboard focus, the chevron also gets a round neutral fill.

### Target size

The header is at least 48px high, and as wide as the panel.

### Screen readers

The header is announced as a button that is expanded or collapsed. The open content is announced as a region, named after the header.

### In the code

- The header has the role `button`, with `aria-expanded` set to `true` or `false`.
- The content has the role `region`, and is named by the header through `aria-labelledby`.
- While the panel is closed, the content has `aria-hidden="true"`, and is not rendered.
- The chevron is decorative and hidden from screen readers.

## In code

The element is `<ha-expansion-panel>`. The content goes in the default slot.

```html
<ha-expansion-panel outlined header="Advanced settings" secondary="Optional">
  <p>...</p>
</ha-expansion-panel>
```

The content only renders while the panel is open. Keep that in mind if something inside needs to exist before it is opened.

### Attributes

- `header`: the header text.
- `secondary`: a short line of text below the header.
- `expanded`: opens the panel. Reflected, and updated when people open or close the panel.
- `outlined`: adds a border and rounded corners.
- `left-chevron`: puts the chevron at the start of the header.
- `no-collapse`: removes the chevron, and the panel can no longer be opened or closed by people. Combine it with `expanded` for a section that looks like a panel but always stays open.

### Slots

- Default: the content.
- `header`: replaces the header text, and the secondary text, with your own content.
- `secondary`: replaces the secondary text.
- `leading-icon`: an icon at the start of the header.
- `icons`: actions at the end of the header, like an icon button.
- `event`: content between the header and the chevron.

### Events

- `expanded-will-change`: fires just before the panel opens or closes. The `detail` has the new `expanded` value.
- `expanded-changed`: fires after the panel opened or closed. The `detail` has the new `expanded` value.

### Styling

- `--expansion-panel-summary-padding`: padding of the header. Defaults to `0 8px`.
- `--expansion-panel-content-padding`: padding of the content. Defaults to `0 8px`.
- `--ha-card-border-radius`: the corner radius of an outlined panel. Defaults to `var(--ha-border-radius-lg)`.

The `summary` part targets the header, for anything the custom properties don't cover.
